## 1. Purpose

180 Degrees Consulting Sheffield CIC (company no. 15874544, registered office 67 Barber Road, Sheffield, England, S10 1EB) is the data controller for 180Connect. This policy sets out what data the platform collects, how it is stored and accessed, how it is protected, how long it is kept and who it is shared with. It is written in line with UK GDPR, The Data Protection Act 2018 and PECR.

Any question about this policy, and any request to exercise a data protection right, goes to **[sheffield@180dc.org](mailto:sheffield@180dc.org)**.

## 2. What Data We Collect

**External (from public registries):**
Charity Commission and Companies House profiles, registration numbers, sector, income and geographic reach, sourced from CharityBase, Companies House, Charity Commission API, Find That Charity and 360Giving.

**Internal (created through platform use):**
Contact history, pipeline stage, CAM ownership, outreach, notes, tags and CAM preferences, including the full email life cycle (drafts, sends, opens, bounces, replies).

**Derived (generated by ML/LLM):**
Priority scores, enrichment summaries, AI-generated email drafts and reply sentiment/intent classification. All derived data keeps its generation date, source and model version.

**What we do not collect:**
* Personal home addresses
* Special category data (Health, Ethnicity, Religion, etc.)
* Any data beyond what an organisation has made public or the minimum professional contact needed for outreach.

This reflects the data minimisation principle: only collecting what is adequate, relevant and limited to what's necessary [UK GDPR Article 5(1)(c)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/5)

**How this exclusion is enforced.** Before live outreach begins, these exclusions
are enforced in code rather than by convention: a field-level rule set, held in
the database and editable by an admin, is applied at the single point where
external data enters the platform. A field the rules exclude is discarded before
it is written, even where the source API returns it, and the record notes which
fields were removed and under which version of the rules. This matters most for
registry endpoints that expose officer and trustee details, where a registered
office address is frequently an individual's home address.

## 3. How the Data is Stored and Accessed

All data lives in Supabase, moving through five trust layers: raw intake → validated core entities → intelligence/scoring → outreach records → read only analytics. Nothing is promoted to a trusted table until it passes validation.

Access is role based: Admin (full access), CAM (own records + shared view), Viewer (read only). Row level security is deny-by-default: access must be explicitly granted.

180Connect is an internal tool. Nobody can create their own account: sign-in is restricted to Google Workspace accounts on approved 180DC domains, and an admin must create the corresponding 180Connect account before that person can reach any data. Both conditions must hold — a Workspace account alone grants nothing. This is enforced in the database, not only in the sign-in screen.

This matches the "integrity and confidentiality" principle, which requires appropriate technical measures to prevent unauthorised access. [UK GDPR Article 5(1)(f)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/5)

**Outreach is sent from an organisational mailbox, not a personal one.** Client
outreach is sent from [sheffield@180dc.org](mailto:sheffield@180dc.org), a Google Workspace account controlled
by 180DC, with replies synced back into the platform (§7). No CAM's personal
mailbox is connected to 180Connect, and no correspondence unrelated to
180Connect outreach is processed. The platform's access to that mailbox is
limited to sending, and to reading only the threads 180Connect itself started;
the limit is enforced by the permissions the account grants, not by convention.
Every send is still attributed in the platform to the individual CAM who took
the action (§4).

The 180dc.org Google Workspace tenant is administered by 180 Degrees Consulting
globally rather than by the Sheffield CIC. Administrators of that tenant can
therefore access the outreach mailbox and its replies. This access path sits
outside the platform's own role-based controls, so it is disclosed here, covered
by the written arrangement at §7, and assessed in the DPIA.

## 4. How Data is Protected

* Emails can only be sent by a CAM who is logged in and takes the action themselves. This is enforced on the backend, not just in the interface, so it can't be bypassed.
* If a charity has asked not to be contacted, the system blocks any further emails to them automatically.
* API keys and login tokens are kept in Vercel and Supabase's secure settings. They're never written into the code itself or pushed to GitHub.
* Every meaningful change, like reassigning a charity or lifting a suppression flag, is recorded with who did it, when, and why.
* The database is backed up daily and backups are retained for 30 days, so the platform can be restored to any day within that window.

These are the "appropriate technical and organisational measures" UK GDPR requires controllers to put in place. [UK GDPR Article 32](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/32)

## 5. Retention and Deletion

Data is kept only as long as needed for its purpose, then deleted or anonymised. The full retention schedule — every table, its trigger event, its period, and the reasoning behind it — is held in [`docs/data-lifecycle-policy.md`](docs/data-lifecycle-policy.md) §6 and is not duplicated here. The periods that matter most:

* Suppression records are kept indefinitely, holding the minimum information needed to permanently honour a do-not-contact request
* Raw source records — unfiltered payloads as returned by a registry API — are kept for **12 months from collection**, then purged. Their purpose is to allow reprocessing after a data-mapping error, which is a short-lived need; a longer period could not be justified for unfiltered third-party data
* Backups roll off after 30 days (§4)
* **Planned — before live outreach begins:** a CAM will be able to request deletion of their own account activity via Settings → Data and Privacy. Until that exists, the route is a request to the platform admin, handled under §6

This is the storage limitation principle: data must not be kept in identifiable form for longer than necessary. [UK GDPR Article 5(1)(e)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/5)

## 6. Lawful Basis and Individual Rights

Outreach relies on legitimate interest, supported by a documented Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA) completed before live outreach begins. The LIA is owned by the Project Manager and must be signed off alongside this policy.

Legitimate interest requires a three part balancing test before it can be relied on. [UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/6)

PECR does not require consent to email a corporate subscriber (most charities/ companies), but UK GDPR still governs any personal data used to make contact ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)).

PECR also governs analytics cookies. Product analytics (§7) will be configured so that no non-essential cookie is set without consent, or run in a cookieless mode where consent is not required.

Every outreach email states who we are, why we are contacting them, where their details came from and how to opt out.

As a registered company, we must also identify ourselves in full on business email: the company name **180 Degrees Consulting Sheffield CIC**, registered number **15874544**, place of registration **England and Wales**, and the registered office address. This sits in the footer of every outreach email alongside the opt-out. ([Companies (Trading Disclosures) Regulations 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/17/regulation/25))

Individuals can request access, correction, objection or erasure of their data.

Access requests must be met within one month of receipt (extendable by up to two months if complex). [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/subject-access-requests/a-guide-to-subject-access/#considerresponding)

**Automated decision-making.** 180Connect generates priority scores and other
derived signals that rank organisations for outreach. These rank and suggest;
they do not decide. Every outreach email is reviewed and sent by a named CAM who
takes the action themselves (§4), so no decision producing legal or similarly
significant effects is taken by automated means alone. [UK GDPR Article 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/22)

**Data Protection Impact Assessment.** The platform profiles organisations and
their contacts at scale using automated scoring, which meets the threshold for a
DPIA. **A DPIA must be completed and signed off before live outreach begins**,
and reviewed whenever a new data source or a new automated decision is
introduced. [UK GDPR Article 35](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/35)

## 8. International Data Transfers

Data stored in the EEA — Supabase and PostHog above — is covered by the UK's adequacy regulations for the EEA and needs no additional safeguard. Where a provider is US-based, or where support staff outside the UK and EEA may access data, the transfer is covered by that provider's data processing agreement incorporating the Standard Contractual Clauses and the ICO's International Data Transfer Addendum. Each provider's hosting region and safeguard is recorded in the table at §7, which serves as the processor register.

No personal data is transferred to a provider before its safeguard is in place and recorded.

Transfers outside the UK require either an adequacy decision or an appropriate safeguard such as the IDTA. [UK GDPR Art. 46](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/46)

## 9. Breach Response

In the event of a data breach, 180Connect will:

- Contain the breach to prevent further access
- Investigate its scope and cause
- Revoke any exposed credentials
- Record the incident in the breach log, whether or not it is notifiable — a dated entry covering what happened, what data and how many people were affected, what was done, and the decision on whether to notify
- Notify the relevant parties, as required by law

The ICO must be notified within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach that risks individuals' rights. [UK GDPR Article 33](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/33)

Affected individuals are told directly, without undue delay, where the risk to them is high. [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach/personal-data-breach/personal-data-breaches-a-guide/)

Recording every breach, including those that are not notifiable, is itself a requirement — the ICO can ask to see the log. [UK GDPR Article 33(5)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/33)
