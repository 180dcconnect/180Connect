180 Degrees Consulting Sheffield CIC (company no. 15874544, registered office 67 Barber Road, Sheffield, England, S10 1EB) is the data controller for 180Connect. ## 2. What Data We Collect

**External (from public registries):**
Charity Commission and Companies House profiles, registration numbers, sector, income, and geographic reach, sourced from CharityBase, Companies House, Charity Commission API, Find That Charity, and 360Giving.

**Internal (created through platform use):**
Contact history, pipeline stage, CAM ownership, outreach records, notes, tags, and CAM preferences, including the full email life cycle (drafts, sends, opens, bounces, replies).

**Derived (generated by ML/analytics):**
Priority scores, enrichment summaries, AI-generated email drafts, and reply classification. All derived data keeps its generation date, source, and model version.

**What we do not collect:**
* Personal home addresses (including director/trustee residential correspondence addresses)
* Special category data (Health, Ethnicity, Religion, Political affiliation, Sexual orientation, etc.)
* Personal email addresses or personal phone numbers
* Any data beyond what an organisation has made public or the minimum professional contact needed for outreach.

This reflects the data minimisation principle: only collecting what is adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary ([UK GDPR Article 5(1)(c)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/5)).

### Public Data Handling Rules (F246 & F247)

180Connect does not treat public availability on third-party APIs or registries as blanket permission to store any information returned.

Before external data is persisted:
1. **Automated Ingestion Filtering:** Every payload passes through a single choke point (`applyDataHandling` in `src/lib/ingestion/apply-data-handling.ts`). Fields classified as excluded under the platform's active rule set (such as officer residential addresses, dates of birth, and trustee names) are stripped before writing.
2. **Redaction of Unstructured Data:** Where personal information appears inside unstructured text or markup (such as scraped web pages), deterministic redaction detectors replace personal email addresses and telephone numbers with redaction placeholders (`[redacted:personal-email]`, `[redacted:phone]`).
3. **Role Email Allow-List:** An allow-list (`personal_email_role_parts`) identifies generic role inboxes (`info@`, `contact@`, `fundraising@`). Addresses not matching a role pattern are treated as personal and excluded.
4. **Manual Entry & URL Import Protection:** A database trigger on `manual_entry_records.contact_email` (`check_manual_entry_contact_email`) rejects any attempt to enter a personal email address, ensuring parity between automated registry feeds and CAM manual entries.
5. **Auditing & Versioning:** All data handling rules are versioned and stored in the database (`data_handling_rules`), and every modification writes an immutable `audit_log` row. The full field-level trace is documented in [`docs/personal-data-exclusions.md`](personal-data-exclusions.md).

## 3. How the Data is Stored and Accessed

All data lives in Supabase, moving through five trust layers: raw intake → validated core entities → intelligence/scoring → outreach records → read-only analytics. Nothing is promoted to a trusted table until it passes validation.

Access is role-based: Admin (full access), CAM (own records + shared view), Viewer (read only). Row level security (RLS) is deny-by-default: access must be explicitly granted.

180Connect is an internal tool. Nobody can create their own account: sign-in is restricted to Google Workspace accounts on approved 180DC domains, and an admin must create the corresponding 180Connect account before that person can reach any data. Both conditions must hold — a Workspace account alone grants nothing. This is enforced in the database, not only in the sign-in screen.

This matches the "integrity and confidentiality" principle, which requires appropriate technical measures to prevent unauthorised access ([UK GDPR Article 5(1)(f)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/5)).

**Outreach mailbox governance:**
* Client outreach is sent from [sheffield@180dc.org](mailto:sheffield@180dc.org), a dedicated Google Workspace account controlled by 180DC Sheffield, with thread replies synced back into the platform (§7).
* No CAM's personal mailbox is connected to 180Connect.
* **Planned / pre-live requirement:** Platform API access to that mailbox is constrained via OAuth scoping to sending outreach and reading only threads initiated by 180Connect.
* Every send is attributed in the platform to the individual CAM who took the action (§4).
* The `180dc.org` Google Workspace tenant is administered jointly with 180 Degrees Consulting globally. The formal governance arrangement for this shared infrastructure is tracked under §7 and §11.

## 4. How Data is Protected

* **Review-before-send:** Emails can only be sent by an authenticated, active CAM who explicitly approves and initiates the send. Sending cannot occur autonomously.
* **Automated suppressions:** If an organisation has requested not to be contacted (do-not-contact flag or suppression record), the system blocks any further outreach automatically at the database level.
* **Secrets management:** API keys and credentials reside in encrypted environment configurations on Vercel and Supabase; they are never committed to version control.
* **Audit trail:** Sensitive mutations (reassigning ownership, modifying roles, adding/removing data rules, lifting suppressions) write immutable audit entries recording actor, timestamp, target, and justification.
* **Database backups:** The database is backed up daily and backups roll off after 30 days.

These represent the appropriate technical and organisational measures required under [UK GDPR Article 32](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/32).

## 5. Retention and Deletion

Data is kept only as long as needed for its purpose, then deleted or anonymised in accordance with the storage limitation principle ([UK GDPR Article 5(1)(e)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/5)).

The full retention schedule is set out in [`docs/data-lifecycle-policy.md`](data-lifecycle-policy.md) §6. Key retention periods include:

* **Raw source records:** Source entity payloads stored in `raw_source_records` have personal data exclusions (F246/F247) applied prior to insertion. They are retained for **12 months from collection** strictly to enable schema re-mapping, data provenance auditing, and discrepancy investigation, after which they are permanently purged.
* **Suppression records:** Retained indefinitely, holding the minimal identifier needed to honour do-not-contact requests permanently.
* **Outreach history & audit logs:** Retained for the operational life of the client relationship, supporting accountability and suppression verification.
* **Database backups:** Roll off automatically after 30 days (§4).
* **Planned / pre-live requirement:** CAM account self-service activity deletion request mechanism via Settings → Data and Privacy. Until live, requests are submitted to the platform admin.

## 6. Lawful Basis and Individual Rights

### Lawful Basis (UK GDPR & PECR)
* **UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f) (Legitimate Interests):** Outreach to prospective social sector clients relies on legitimate interests, supported by a documented Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA). The LIA evaluates the legitimate business interest of the CIC against the rights of individual contacts. The LIA must be signed off prior to live outreach.
* **PECR (Direct Marketing):** PECR permits direct marketing communications to corporate bodies (including incorporated charities, CICs, and limited companies) without prior consent. Where communications address an identifiable individual at a corporate body, UK GDPR applies to that personal data.
* **Sole traders and individual subscribers:** 180Connect must distinguish corporate subscribers from individual subscribers for PECR purposes. Sole traders and certain types of partnership are treated as individual subscribers and must not receive direct marketing emails unless an applicable PECR lawful route exists, such as valid consent or the applicable soft opt-in. Corporate subscribers may be contacted by email without prior consent under PECR, subject to the applicable UK GDPR requirements where personal data is processed.
* **Trading Disclosures:** In accordance with company-law trading disclosure requirements, all outreach communications include the statutory company details (**180 Degrees Consulting Sheffield CIC**, company number **15874544**, registered in **England and Wales**, registered office address), sender identity, contact address, and a clear opt-out mechanism.

### Decision-Support & Article 22
* 180Connect generates priority scores and enrichment summaries to assist CAMs.
* **These signals are decision-support tools only; they do not decide.**
* A CAM must independently review the organisation details and contact suitability, has full authority to reject the suggestion, and must affirmatively trigger any email send. No automated decision producing legal or similarly significant effects occurs ([UK GDPR Article 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/22)).

### Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)
* The processing has been screened against the ICO's high-risk criteria and is treated as requiring a DPIA because it combines automated scoring/profiling, data matching across multiple external sources, indirect data collection, and AI technologies.
* **A DPIA must be completed and approved before live outreach begins** ([UK GDPR Article 35](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/35)).

### Transparency & Privacy Notice (Article 14)
* Because contact information is obtained indirectly from public registers and corporate websites, an Article 14 compliant Privacy Notice explaining data sources, processing purposes, lawful bases, retention, profiling, and rights will be published and linked in outreach footers before live outreach begins.

### Individual Rights
Individuals have the right to request access, rectification, erasure, restriction, or objection:
* **Right to Object (Article 21):** Objection to direct marketing is absolute; requests result in immediate and permanent suppression.
* **Subject Access Requests (SARs):** Handled within one calendar month (extendable by two months for complex requests).
* Requests are managed via **[sheffield@180dc.org](mailto:sheffield@180dc.org)**.

### LLM Processing Rules
1. **Pre-Approval Gate:** No personal data may be transmitted to an LLM provider unless the provider, purpose, DPA, data retention terms (confirming prompts are not used for model training), processing region, and transfer mechanisms have been formally approved.
2. **Data Minimisation:** LLM prompts must contain only the minimum organisational text necessary for the task.
3. **Prohibition on Special Category Data:** Special category data and excluded personal data must never be submitted to an LLM.
4. **Incoming Correspondence Screening:** Incoming client correspondence (e.g. replies containing unprompted personal health disclosures or personal circumstances) is not automatically safe and must not be submitted to third-party LLMs without prior redaction.

## 9. Breach Response

In the event of a personal data breach, 180Connect will:
1. Contain the incident immediately to prevent further exposure.
2. Investigate the cause, scope, and categories of data subjects affected.
3. Revoke compromised credentials or access tokens.
4. Record the incident in the internal Breach Log in accordance with [UK GDPR Article 33(5)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/33), whether or not the breach meets the notification threshold.
5. Notify the ICO without undue delay and, where feasible, within **72 hours** of becoming aware of the breach, **where the breach is likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons** ([UK GDPR Article 33](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/33)).
6. Notify affected individuals without undue delay where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms ([UK GDPR Article 34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/34)).
